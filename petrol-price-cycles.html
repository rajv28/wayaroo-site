Petrol price cycles in Australia, and when to fill up

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 20 November 2026.

If you drive in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide or Perth, you have seen it: one week unleaded is 179.9, a few days later the same station shows 209.9. This is not random. Petrol prices in these five cities move in a price cycle. If you understand the cycle, you can save a few dollars on every tank without driving across town.

What a price cycle is

In a price cycle, prices go up sharply in one or two days, then come down slowly over several weeks, until the next jump. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC), which monitors fuel prices, says the cycles come from the pricing decisions of the retailers themselves. They do not follow day-to-day changes in wholesale prices or costs, and not every retailer takes part.

A cycle has three parts:

  • The low point (the trough). Prices have drifted down for weeks. This is the best time to buy.
  • The jump (the restoration). Large retailers raise prices at many stations within a day or two. Independent stations often follow a little later.
  • The fall (discounting). Stations cut prices a little at a time to win customers, and the average comes down until the next jump.

How long a cycle lasts in each city

The ACCC publishes how long the cycles were. Its averages for 2025 were:

CityAverage cycle in 2025
Perthabout 1 week
Adelaideabout 2.5 weeks
Sydneyabout 5 weeks
Melbourneabout 6 weeks
Brisbaneabout 6.5 weeks

Cycles change from year to year, and sometimes they stop. The ACCC notes that since the conflict in the Middle East started in late February 2026, cycles have mostly not occurred in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Adelaide. When oil prices move a lot, the normal pattern can disappear for months.

Where there is no cycle

Canberra, Hobart, Darwin and most regional towns do not have regular cycles. There, prices follow wholesale costs more slowly, and the difference between stations matters more than the day you buy. Diesel and LPG also do not follow the cycle, even in the big cities. For these, compare stations near you instead of waiting for a good day.

Simple rules to pay less

  1. Know where you are in the cycle. If prices near you have dropped for two or three weeks, a jump is likely soon in Sydney, Melbourne or Brisbane. Fill up now. If prices jumped yesterday, buy only what you need and wait.
  2. In Perth, use the day. The weekly cycle makes the day matter. Western Australia's FuelWatch also publishes tomorrow's prices after 2:30 pm each day, because stations must set them a day ahead.
  3. Compare the stations near you. On the same day, stations a few kilometres apart can differ by 20 cents a litre or more. Independent brands are often cheaper during the jump.
  4. Do the sum before you drive. A 15-cent saving on 40 litres is $6. If the cheaper station is 10 km away and back, your car burns about 2 litres (about $4) to get there. The saving is real only when the station is on your way or close.
  5. Do not wait until the light comes on. An empty tank forces you to buy at the top of the cycle. A tank that is a quarter full gives you time to wait for a better price.
