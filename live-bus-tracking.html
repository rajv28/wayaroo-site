How live bus and train tracking works

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 20 November 2026.

You open a map, and a small bus moves along the road towards your stop. How does the app know where the bus is? This page explains, in plain words, where live public transport data comes from, why the dot sometimes jumps or disappears, and how to read the times so you do not miss your ride.

Step 1: the vehicle knows where it is

Most buses, trams and trains in Australian cities carry a GPS unit, often in the ticket machine or the driver's console. Every 10 to 30 seconds it sends its position, the trip it is running and the time to the transport agency's computers.

Step 2: the agency publishes it as open data

Transport agencies such as Transport Victoria and Transport for NSW publish this data in a shared world format called GTFS (General Transit Feed Specification). It has two parts:

  • The timetable (GTFS): every route, stop and planned time. It changes every few weeks.
  • The live part (GTFS-Realtime): where each vehicle is now (vehicle positions), how late each trip is at each coming stop (trip updates), and alerts such as diversions.

Any app may use this data under an open licence, as long as it names the source. That is why many apps can show the same buses.

Step 3: the app joins the two

The app matches each live vehicle to its trip in the timetable. Then it can show the route number, where the bus is going, and the predicted time at each coming stop. Wayaroo reads the live feeds every few seconds and draws each vehicle on the map.

Why the dot is sometimes wrong

  • Old position. A vehicle sends its position only every 10 to 30 seconds, so the dot can be a little behind.
  • No signal. In tunnels and the City Loop, trains lose GPS. The dot can stop or jump.
  • Not tracked. Some vehicles have no working tracker on some days. The app then can show only the timetable time.
  • Wrong trip. Sometimes a vehicle is logged on to the wrong trip, so it shows the wrong route for a while.

How to read live times

  • "Live" or a moving dot: the time is a prediction from where the vehicle is now. It is usually good to a minute or two.
  • "Scheduled": there is no live data for this trip. The vehicle may be early, late, or not running.
  • Be at the stop early. Buses can leave a stop early when the road is clear. Aim to arrive two or three minutes before the predicted time.

Where Wayaroo has live vehicles
