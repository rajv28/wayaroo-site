EV charging in Australia: plugs and speeds explained

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 22 November 2026.

Charging an electric car is easy once you know three things: the plug your car uses, the difference between AC and DC charging, and how the charging speed changes. This page explains them for Australia, where most chargers now use the same two plug types.

AC and DC

The grid supplies alternating current (AC), but a car battery stores direct current (DC). Every electric car has an on-board charger that turns AC into DC. When you plug into an AC charger, the car's own on-board charger sets the limit. A DC fast charger does the conversion itself and sends DC straight to the battery, which is why it is much faster.

The plugs

How fast is each charger?

  • A normal power point at home is the slowest. It suits cars that drive short distances and sit overnight.
  • Level 2 AC chargers (a wall box at home, or at shopping centres and car parks) typically supply 7 to 22 kW. A few hours adds a lot of range.
  • DC fast chargers (often 50 kW to 350 kW) suit road trips. Twenty to forty minutes can add a few hundred kilometres, depending on the car.

The number on the charger is its maximum. The real speed also depends on your car's limit, the battery's temperature and how full it is: charging slows down as the battery gets close to full. On a road trip, it is often quicker to charge to about 80 per cent and stop again later than to wait for 100 per cent.

Cost

Charging at home on your normal electricity plan is usually the cheapest. Public AC chargers are often cheap or free at shopping centres and councils. DC fast charging costs more per kilowatt-hour, and some networks also charge an idle fee if your car stays plugged in after it is full. Check the price in the network's app before you plug in.

Tips for charging away from home

  • Check the plug type, the power and whether the charger is working before you drive to it.
  • Have a backup charger in mind on long country trips, where sites are far apart.
  • Move your car when it is charged, so the next driver can use the bay.
  • Carry your Type 2 cable for AC chargers.
