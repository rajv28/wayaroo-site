School zone times in Australia, state by state

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 20 November 2026.

A school zone is a stretch of road near a school with a lower speed limit, usually 40 km/h, at the times children arrive and leave. The times are not the same in every state, and a driver who crosses a border can be caught out. This page lists the usual times and the rules that matter.

The usual times

State or territoryUsual school zone times
Victoria8:00 to 9:30 am and 2:30 to 4:00 pm on school days
New South Wales8:00 to 9:30 am and 2:30 to 4:00 pm on school days; signs have flashing lights
QueenslandMost zones 7:00 to 9:00 am and 2:00 to 4:00 pm; some schools differ
South Australia25 km/h zones: at any time when a child is in the zone. New 40 km/h school precincts (being installed from late October 2025 to late 2026): 8:00 to 9:30 am and 2:00 to 4:00 pm on school days
Western AustraliaMost zones 7:30 to 9:00 am and 2:00 to 4:00 pm (Kimberley: 7:00 to 8:30 am and 2:00 to 3:30 pm)
Tasmania8:00 to 9:30 am and 2:30 to 4:00 pm; signs light up during the times
Australian Capital Territory8:00 am to 4:00 pm on school days
Northern Territory7:00 am to 5:00 pm on school days

The sign at the zone is always the rule. Some schools have different times, and some zones apply on other days. If the sign shows times, those times apply; if an electronic sign shows 40, then 40 applies.

School days, holidays and pupil-free days

In the states with fixed times, the lower limit applies on school days. On school holidays and weekends the normal limit applies, unless an electronic sign shows the lower limit. Pupil-free days are a common trap: children can still be near the school, and some signs still show the school limit. When in doubt, slow down.

South Australia is different

In South Australia the 25 km/h school zones on local streets apply 24 hours a day, every day, whenever a child is in the zone: you must look for children, not at the clock. South Australia is also adding 40 km/h school precincts with set times on school days (8:00 to 9:30 am and 2:00 to 4:00 pm). Each one applies once its signs are up.

What else to watch for

  • School crossings. When the crossing flags are out or a supervisor shows the stop sign, stop and stay stopped until everyone has crossed.
  • Buses. Some states have a 40 km/h limit when you pass a school bus that shows flashing lights. Check your state's rules.
  • Cameras. Fixed and mobile speed cameras are often placed in school zones. Fines and demerit points for school zone offences can be higher than on other roads.

How Wayaroo helps

In Victoria, Wayaroo knows the school zones from the state's open data. While you drive, the speedometer shows the school zone limit at school times on school days, and a voice says it once as you enter the zone. Outside those times, it shows the normal limit. Elsewhere in Australia, the speedometer shows the mapped limit. Always follow the signs: GPS and maps can be wrong.
