Where speed limits come from, and how to report a wrong one

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 20 November 2026.

A navigation app shows a speed limit for almost every road. Most of the time it is correct. Sometimes it is not: a new sign went up last week, roadworks lowered the limit, or the map has an old value. This page explains where Wayaroo's speed limits come from, why a limit can be wrong, and how you can report it without breaking the road rules.

The sign on the road is the law

In Victoria you must drive at or below the limit on the speed limit signs. Where there is no sign, a default limit applies: 50 km/h on roads in built-up areas, and 100 km/h on country roads outside built-up areas. A temporary limit at roadworks applies even when you cannot see any work. When you pass an emergency, incident response or roadside assistance vehicle that is stopped or very slow and has flashing lights or an alarm, you must slow down to 40 km/h.

An app can only help you to know the limit. When the app and the sign are different, obey the sign. Default limits and school zone times are different in other states and territories: check the road authority of the state where you drive.

Where Wayaroo's limits come from

  • In Victoria: the official speed zones. The Department of Transport and Planning publishes the legal speed limit of every road section as open data, with the school zones. Wayaroo reads the whole file again each month.
  • Elsewhere: OpenStreetMap. Outside Victoria, or where the official data has no limit, Wayaroo uses the limit that OpenStreetMap volunteers recorded for the nearest road, up to about 35 metres away.
  • Roadworks come first. When Transport Victoria publishes roadworks or an incident with a lower speed at your place, the speedometer shows that lower limit. When the notice gives no number, it shows a roadworks sign with no number.
  • School zones only at school times. In Victoria, the lower limit of a time-based school zone shows only on school days, from 8 to 9:30 am and from 2:30 to 4 pm, and not on weekends, public holidays or school holidays. Some school zones apply at all times, and electronic signs can show other limits at other times: follow the sign.

Why a limit can be wrong

  • A road authority changed the limit, and the open data or the map does not have the change yet.
  • OpenStreetMap is made by volunteers. A road can have no limit recorded, or an old one.
  • Near a junction, or where two roads run side by side, the phone's position can match the wrong road for a short time.
  • Temporary limits at roadworks, at events and on electronic signs change faster than any data.

What the road rules say about your phone

In Victoria, a driver with a full licence may use a phone that is in a commercially made holder for navigation, and may touch it briefly to use a navigation function. A driver must not type or enter numbers, also when the car is stopped at a red light. Learner and probationary drivers have stricter rules. So a report that a driver can make must need no typing and no choice of a number.

How to report a wrong limit in Wayaroo

  • While you drive: one button. Tap the speedometer once. Wayaroo shows one big button, "This limit is wrong". It sends the place, the direction (when the phone knows it), the limit that the app showed and the time. You type nothing and you choose no number.
  • Parked, or as a passenger: the correct number. When the phone stands still, or when you told Wayaroo that you are a passenger, Wayaroo shows a few road signs (for example 40, 50 and 60). Choose the one that is on the real sign.
  • Your phone uses your number. After you choose a number, your phone shows your limit near that place, in that direction, for 90 days. Other people do not see it. Roadworks and school zones at school times still come first.
  • Wayaroo checks each report. A report has no name and is deleted after 14 days. Wayaroo corrects the map's data only after a check.
  • Tell OpenStreetMap too. After you choose a number, and when the limit came from OpenStreetMap or there was none, Wayaroo asks if you want to send a public note to OpenStreetMap. Volunteers can then correct the map for everyone who uses it. The note shows the place and the time, with no name.
