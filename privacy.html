Wayaroo

Privacy

Last updated 25 November 2026.

The short version

You need no account for Wayaroo (one is optional, see Account below), and Wayaroo itself doesn't track you: your trips and settings stay on your phone. In the app, the advertising is a clearly labelled Sponsored spot from a local business, a sponsor's name as a pin on the map of a car's screen, a Google ad in the More menu, and one full-screen Google ad when you open the iPhone app (at most once each time the app starts, not on your first day with the app, and never while a trip runs or the phone is connected to a car's screen). No pop-up, banner or full-screen ad shows while you drive. The information website wayaroo.com shows Google ads (see below); the map at app.wayaroo.com does not.

Your location

If you allow it, your phone's location is used in the app to centre the map, plan a trip from where you are, and record a drive. Wayaroo does not keep your location on its server. When you plan a route, the start and end points (and, when the app plans again during a drive, the direction you are going) are sent to the routing service to work out the route, and are not stored by Wayaroo. Nearby things (stops, fuel, toilets, weather) are looked up by sending the Wayaroo server the position you are looking at; a flight you search for is looked up by its number. None of that is kept.

Share my trip

If you tap Share my trip, your position and expected arrival time are sent to the Wayaroo server about every 30 seconds and shown to anyone who opens your link. The link is random and private to the people you give it to. The server keeps this only in memory, never on disk, and forgets it when you end the trip or after 12 hours at the latest. Share the link only with people you trust.

Share this bus (a school bus without live positions) works the same way from the phone of the adult on board who starts it: only the bus’s position is sent, about every 20 seconds while Wayaroo is open, with the route number as the link’s label. Nothing about anyone on board is sent or kept, and the share ends when it is stopped, when Wayaroo is closed, or after 3 hours at the latest.

Family & friends map

Only if you start or join a group (More → Family & friends map). The group sees the name you type and, while "Share my location" is on and Wayaroo is open, where you are about every 45 seconds. Positions are kept in memory for at most 2 hours and never saved; the group's names are saved so it still works tomorrow. There's no chat. With your position the group also sees your phone's battery level (rounded to 5 %, and whether it is charging), whether you are stopped, walking or driving (speed rounded to 5 km/h) and the emoji you picked; these are kept in memory with the position. The group can save up to 5 places (Home, School, Work, Gym or Other, with a point on the map); places are saved with the group until someone removes them, and each phone works out "arrived" and "left" by itself. SOS and "I'm OK" send the time and where you are once, even when sharing is paused; they are kept in memory for 30 minutes (SOS) or 2 hours (I'm OK). SOS does not call 000 or any emergency service. You can also pick a status from a fixed list (for example "Driving" or "Busy, call later"); the group sees it for 12 hours. Pause sharing or leave the group at any time; the group is deleted when its last member leaves.

Helping other riders see your bus or train

Off unless you turn it on (on the ride card, "Help other riders"). While Wayaroo is guiding you on a bus, train, tram or ferry, your phone sends its position and the line number about every 8 seconds, with a random number made for that ride only — no name, account or phone ID. We use it only to show that vehicle in the right place on the live map, keep it in memory for under a minute, and never save or log it. Tap "Stop" on the ride card to turn it off.

Things you tell other riders and drivers

When you tap This one is full on a vehicle, or type a fuel price you saw at the pump, Wayaroo stores what you reported (the route, stop or station, and the time) and uses it to help other people. It does not store your name or location with it. In the iPhone app you can take a photo of a fuel station's price board to fill in the prices: the photo is read on your phone, it is not sent to Wayaroo or to anyone else and Wayaroo does not keep it; only the prices you then choose to share are sent. To stop abuse, a count of such reports per internet address is kept in memory for an hour, then dropped.

Trips, fuel spend and settings

Saved trips (with the work or personal mark that you give a drive), the odometer reading that you type in for the car logbook, your car's fuel use, language and map settings are kept in your browser on this device only. A logbook or trips file that you export is saved on your device and goes only where you send it. You can delete trips at any time in Spend, or clear them by clearing this site's data in your browser.

Account (optional)

You do not need an account: everything in Wayaroo works as a guest. If you choose to sign in (Settings → Account), Apple or Google only tells Wayaroo that it is you. Wayaroo keeps a scrambled id made from that, not your email address and not your name. An account keeps one thing on the Wayaroo server: the copy of your settings that you save there with "Save my settings to my account", so that a new phone can get it. That copy can contain your home, your work, your saved places and the key of your Family & friends group. It is saved only when you tap the button, and each save replaces the copy before it. Your device keeps a sign-in key, so that you stay signed in for up to 180 days. In a browser the Google sign-in button comes from Google (accounts.google.com) and is loaded when you open Settings as a guest. To delete the account and the copy: Settings → Account → Delete my account. "Sign out" keeps the copy for the next time you sign in.

How many people use Wayaroo

Once a day the app tells the Wayaroo server one word (new, this week, regular or returned), whether it is the app or the website, and the name of your phone's time zone (for example "Australia/Melbourne"). The server adds one to that day's total and one to the total of that time zone. It does not store an ID, your IP address or your location with it. A time zone covers a whole state or country, so it does not show where you are.

The server also counts how many searches and how many route plans it answers each day. It keeps the two totals only: not the words you searched for, not the places, not an ID and not your IP address.

Error reports

When something in the app goes wrong, the app (or the browser that shows it) sends the Wayaroo server a short note: what went wrong, where in the app's code, and whether it is the iPhone app, the Android app or a website. It is sent at most 3 times a visit. It has no name, no ID and no place: web addresses and numbers that could be a position are taken out first. The server does not keep your IP address with it, keeps the notes for 2 days at most, and counts them by kind, so that we can find and fix faults.

Traffic-light waiting times

When you stop at a traffic light while driving, the app asks the Wayaroo server which light is at that position or ahead of it on your road (it sends the position and the direction you were driving in), to show how long people usually wait there. When you move again, the app sends how many seconds you waited, which light it was, the time band (for example weekday peak) and the side you came from (north, east, south or west). The server keeps only the seconds of the last 40 waits for each light, time band and side, to work out the usual wait and the longest usual wait. It does not store your name, an ID, your IP address or your trip with it.

Road speeds

Only after you say Yes. Wayaroo asks you once, when the car stands still (after a drive, or when a car trip starts), never while it moves. Until then nothing is sent; you can change your answer in Settings → Driving → "Help with live traffic". On the car's screen the same answer counts. While a drive is on, your phone sends a few road-speed samples to the Wayaroo server about every 30 seconds: a position on the road (to about 1 metre), the direction and the speed. There is no name, account, phone ID or route with them. The first 400 metres and the last 400 metres of each drive are never sent, so where a drive starts and ends is left out. The server keeps the samples in memory only, for 20 minutes, to colour routes green, yellow or red for other drivers, and then forgets them; nothing is written to disk. To count different vehicles it uses a short code made from the internet address and the hour; the address itself is not kept with the samples. When a route is on the map, the app asks the server for the measured speeds in the area of that route.

Map corrections

When you tap The map is wrong here and send a note, Wayaroo sends your words and the place you chose to OpenStreetMap as a public map note, so volunteers can correct the map. The note is public and has no name on it. Do not write personal details in it. Wayaroo does not keep the note.

Reports you send

When you report a pothole, hazard, faulty traffic light or wrong fuel price, Wayaroo stores the type, the place on the map and the time (for a fuel price, also the price), shows the report to other Wayaroo users and deletes it after 14 days. Each report on the map carries a short code that changes every day: it lets you hide one person’s reports without an account, and reports from different days cannot be linked to the same person. A report has no note or photo, so nothing anyone types is shown to others. Wayaroo does not store your name, account or IP address with a report (a scrambled form of the internet address is kept with points, only to stop people confirming their own reports).

When you tell Wayaroo that a speed limit is wrong, Wayaroo stores the place, the direction you were going (if the phone knows it), the limit you chose (or none), the limit the app showed and the time, for Wayaroo to check. This report has no name or code of yours, is not shown to other users and is deleted after 14 days. When you choose the limit from where you are, it is also kept on your phone for 90 days, so the app shows it at that place; a pin on the map does not keep it, unless it finishes a report that you sent from that place. A report with no number, sent from where you are, keeps its place, direction and time on your phone for 3 hours, so that you can give the number when you have parked. If you then tap Send a public note, Wayaroo sends to OpenStreetMap, as a public map note, the place (to about 10 metres), the limit you chose, the limit OpenStreetMap has there (if any) and the direction, with no name and no words of yours. OpenStreetMap shows the note with its time, so volunteers can correct the map. Wayaroo keeps only the note’s place, limit, direction and time, in memory for up to 30 days, so that the same note is not sent twice.

Crash alerts

While you drive, Wayaroo watches your phone's motion sensor and speed on the phone itself. If it notices a hard hit and then the car stands, it asks "Are you OK?" for 60 seconds. If nobody answers and you are in a Family & friends group with "Crash alerts" on, the group gets an SOS marked "possible crash" with where you are (kept in memory for 30 minutes, as any SOS). In the iPhone app the app keeps your group's key on the phone so it can send that SOS itself. Nothing else is sent, and Wayaroo never calls an emergency number by itself. Switch it off in the Family & friends sheet.

Windscreen camera

Only in the iPhone app and only while you switch it on (More → Windscreen camera), or on each drive after you switched on “Start by itself when I drive” there (it is off until you do): the camera records your drive on your phone in 1-minute parts, keeps the newest 4 minutes and deletes older parts, and reads speed signs on the phone with Apple's own text reading. No picture of the camera shows on the screen, and the camera works only while Wayaroo is on the screen. Nothing is sent anywhere. "Save", or a crash that your phone notices, copies the last 3 minutes to your Photos (iOS asks you first).

Your day

Only if you switch it on (Profile → Your day): while Wayaroo is open, the places you go are kept on your phone only, for 2, 7 or 30 days, and then deleted by themselves. They are never sent. Your phone works out from them, by itself, your stops and the moves between them (walk, drive or on the move, km, top speed), and names a stop Home or Work from your own saved places; no address is looked up. "Save as Home" or "Save as Work" keeps that stop as a saved place on your phone. "Delete all" removes them at once.

Apple Health

Only in the iPhone app and only when you tap “Import from Apple Health” or “Cycling insights” in Move. Import reads your walks, runs and rides (with their routes) and keeps them in Move like the ones you record. Cycling insights reads your cycling workouts, your heart rate during and just after each ride, cadence and power when a sensor recorded them, active energy and distance, and each day’s resting heart rate, heart rate variability, heart rate recovery and VO2 max. It works everything out on your phone and does not store or send any of it; the max heart rate or age you may type and the place names you give rides stay on your phone only (not in backups). Nothing is written to Health, and nothing from Health is sent to Wayaroo or anyone else. The numbers are estimates from your watch, not medical advice. To stop it, go to iPhone Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Wayaroo and switch the items off.

Safe driving and savings scores

Safe driving scores and money-saved totals stay on your phone. A drive is scored from your phone's GPS (hard braking, hard acceleration, hard corners, time over the speed limit) and from touches on the screen while the car moves; on the car's screen (CarPlay) the iPhone app scores the drive and gives it to Wayaroo on the same phone. Only if you switch on "Share my driving score" in a Family & friends group, that group sees your safe driving score of the last 7 days, how far you drove (to 5 km) and how many drives: no routes, no places and no single event. The server keeps it in memory for at most 8 days and never saves it; switch it off and it is taken away at once.

Move and Budget stay on your phone too: the walks, runs and rides you record (with best efforts and fitness numbers), the payments you type in, and a bank statement you bring in as a file (its transactions, balance and the categories you pick). Wayaroo never asks for your bank login. The kilometre voice uses your phone's own voice.

Points and leaderboard

To give you points, your phone makes a random code and keeps it in this browser. The Wayaroo server stores only a scrambled (hashed) form of that code, your nickname (random until you change it), your points, streak and badges, and which reports you checked, so nobody can check the same report twice. Nicknames and points are shown on the public leaderboard. To start again, clear this site's data in your browser.

Parking timer

Where you parked, your time limit, price and note stay in your browser on this device. Wayaroo doesn't take payments; the links open the official parking apps, which have their own privacy policies.

AI answers (off until you turn them on)

In Ask you can switch on AI answers. Until you do, every question is worked out on your phone and nothing is sent anywhere. When it is on and you ask something Wayaroo doesn't already understand, your question and the facts on your screen (live vehicles, departures, weather, reports and works around the area you are looking at) are sent through the Wayaroo server to one AI provider, which writes a short answer from those facts only. If the question is about a trip you are planning or driving (for example by voice in the car), Wayaroo also sends that trip with it: the start, the destination, the route, where you are on it and your speed. The Ask screen names that provider before you switch it on: on the test server it is Thrift, software on the same computer as Wayaroo, which keeps the question there or passes it to a free online model depending on its own privacy setting; on the public server it is the free-tier provider named in the app. Wayaroo does not keep your questions or the answers, and never sends your account or contacts. It sends your exact position only as part of a trip question, as described above. Every answer is marked ✨ AI answer with the provider's name, and it can be wrong, so check it before you rely on it.

Your own AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok or DeepSeek)

In Ask you can also use an AI you already have. The app buttons copy your question and the facts on your screen and open that company's app or website, where you are signed in with your own account; Wayaroo sends nothing for this. If you instead paste an API key from one of these companies, the key is kept only on this phone (it is not in Wayaroo backups). Each question then goes with the key through the Wayaroo server to that company, which answers under its own terms and privacy policy and may charge your API account. The Wayaroo server does not store or log the key, your questions or the answers. Tap Disconnect to delete the key from the phone.

You on the map

The character that shows you on the map is worked out on your phone from its motion sensor and GPS speed (walking, cycling, driving and so on); nobody else sees it. If you give it your own face, a Memoji sticker or a photo, the picture is shrunk to a small square and kept only on your phone (and in a backup file if you save one). Wayaroo never uploads it.

Partner links

Some cards have buttons that open a partner's own app or website, for example Uber or DiDi to the destination of your trip, a car park booking site on a parking card, or tours and places to stay on a place's profile. Only what you tapped goes into the link (such as the destination), and only when you tap it; Wayaroo sends partners nothing else about you. Once you are on their app or site, their terms and privacy policy apply. Wayaroo counts how many times each partner button is tapped each day, with nothing about who tapped. Some of these links can earn Wayaroo a commission, and the card says so. Partner buttons never show while you are driving or navigating.

Other services

Your browser loads map images from OpenFreeMap and the map engine from unpkg (or, when unpkg does not answer, from jsDelivr), so those services see your internet address, like any website. The same goes for things you choose to open: traffic camera photos come straight from the road authority, radio plays straight from the station, NASA photos come from NASA, and 3D hills come from Amazon's open terrain tiles. Searches, routes, weather and other data are fetched by the Wayaroo server for you, so those providers see the Wayaroo server rather than you.

Sponsored spots (ads)

Wayaroo is free because local businesses can pay to show one clearly labelled Sponsored spot. To choose one, the app sends the Wayaroo server only what you just searched for and the map's rough position (rounded to about 1 km). Wayaroo doesn't build a profile of you, doesn't track you across other apps or websites, and doesn't sell or share your information with advertisers. The server only counts how many times each ad was shown and tapped each day. Sponsored cards and Google ads are never shown while you're driving or navigating, or when the phone moves faster than 1 km/h. On a car's screen (Apple CarPlay), a sponsor can show only as a name on the map, at its place; for this the app sends only the car's rough position (rounded to about 1 km).

Google AdMob ads in the iPhone and Android apps are non-personalised: Google doesn't use your activity in other apps and websites to choose them. The exception is your own choice on an iPhone: newer versions of the iPhone app ask once, with Apple's own “Allow tracking?” question, whether Google may use your iPhone's advertising identifier to show ads that match your interests. Wayaroo asks at your first start of the app, after the iPhone's other permission questions (a phone that already has Wayaroo is asked once, at a start of the app), and never while you drive. You can say no, and you can change your answer at any time in the iPhone's Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking; nothing in Wayaroo changes either way, and Wayaroo still never sends Google your trips, places or exact location. Google may still collect device and usage information to show and measure ads; see Google's ad policies.

Google ads on the website wayaroo.com. The pages of wayaroo.com (not the map at app.wayaroo.com and not the apps) can show Google AdSense ads. Google and its partners use cookies and your internet address to show and measure these ads, and can use them to show ads based on your earlier visits to this and other websites. You can switch personalised ads off at Google Ads Settings, and read how Google uses the data at How Google uses information from sites that use its services. On a device whose time zone is in Europe (the European Union and the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland included), the pages of wayaroo.com do not load Google’s ads at all, so no ad cookies are set there.

Contact and complaints

Questions, requests to see or delete information you sent (such as a report), or complaints: email support@wayaroo.com. If you're not happy with the answer you can contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

Who runs Wayaroo

Wayaroo is published by Ozrsym Pty Ltd, Victoria, Australia, and aims to follow the Australian Privacy Principles.
