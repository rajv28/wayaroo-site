Parking signs in Victoria explained

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 22 November 2026.

Most parking fines in Melbourne come from a few simple mistakes. The City of Melbourne says 97 per cent of the fines it issues are for parking too long, not paying, stopping in a No Stopping area, or stopping where the driver is not allowed. This page explains the signs you will see in Victoria and the rules that apply even where there is no sign.

Reading a parking sign

  • The number before "P" is the time limit in hours. "1P" is 1 hour and "2P" is 2 hours; "1/4P" is 15 minutes and "1/2P" is 30 minutes.
  • The times and days under it say when the limit applies. Outside those times, the limit does not apply, unless another panel says something else.
  • "Meter" or "Ticket" means you must also pay, at the meter or the machine the sign points to. A ticket must be displayed so it can be read from outside.
  • The time limit starts when you stop. Paying does not start, restart or extend it. To stay longer, you must move to another parking area.

The signs that cause most fines

SignWhat it means
No Stopping (red circle, "S" with a line through it)You must not stop for any reason, even for a moment. It is an offence even with the engine running, people in the car or the hazard lights on.
No Parking (red circle, "P" with a line through it)You may stop for up to 2 minutes to drop off or pick up people or goods, and you must stay within 3 metres of the vehicle.
ClearwayNo stopping at the times shown, so that the lane can carry traffic. Vehicles left in a clearway are often towed.
Loading ZoneOnly for eligible vehicles that are loading or unloading. Ordinary passenger cars must not stop there for any reason. With no time on the sign, the limit is 30 minutes.
Permit ZoneOnly for vehicles that display the permit named on the sign, for example a resident permit for one area.
Taxi ZoneOnly taxis. Ride-share cars, such as Uber, must not stop there, even for a moment.
Disability parkingOnly with a valid Australian Disability Parking Permit on display, and the permit holder must be the driver or a passenger. The time limit on the sign still applies.

Rules that need no sign

  • Intersections: do not park within 20 metres of an intersection with traffic lights, or within 10 metres of any other intersection.
  • Nature strips and footpaths: do not stop on them for any reason. Only motorcycles may park on a footpath, and only where they do not block people.
  • Driveways: you may stop across a driveway for up to 2 minutes to drop off or pick up passengers, but you must not park across any part of it, even your own.
  • Direction: park facing the same way as the traffic in the lane next to you.

Signs with more than one panel

Many signs have two or three panels for different times, for example "2P Meter 7am–4pm" above "Clearway 4pm–7pm". Read every panel, and check that a later panel will not start while you are parked.

Public holidays

In Victoria, a sign that names any day of the week (for example "2P Meter Mon–Fri 7am–10pm") does not apply on a public holiday. You can park there with no time limit and no fee. A sign that names no day, such as a permit zone, a bus zone or a disability space, applies every day, including public holidays. The City of Melbourne also applies the public holiday rule on a day in lieu.
