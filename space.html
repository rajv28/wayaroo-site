From your street to space, in one smooth zoom

Wayaroo is a map first. Pinch out from your street and the city gets small. Keep going and you see the whole round Earth. Keep going again and you leave Earth behind: the Moon, the Sun and the planets come into view.

The Solar System, where it really is today

Out in space, the Sun, the Moon and the planets sit where they really are on today's date. Wayaroo works out their places on your phone from orbit numbers published by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL).

  • A "You are here" marker shows where you are on Earth.
  • Tap the Moon, the Sun or a planet to see how far away it is from you, for example "the Moon is 369,649 km from you". The number changes every day.
  • Fly to a planet for a closer look.

Speed up time

Press play and time goes faster. Watch the Moon go round the Earth in seconds, and the planets move along their orbits over weeks, months and years. Go back to today with one tap.

Space in big numbers

  • 8:20minutes and seconds

    About how long sunlight takes to reach Earth. When you look at the Sun's light, you see it as it was a little over 8 minutes ago.

  • 25+billion km

    Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is now more than 25 billion km away: the farthest spacecraft from Earth.

  • 384,400km

    The average distance to the Moon. Its path round Earth is not a perfect circle, so the distance changes every day.

  • 150million km

    About how far Earth is from the Sun. Scientists call this distance one astronomical unit.

Positions from NASA/JPL Horizons

Spacecraft, where they are now

See spacecraft on their way through the Solar System, such as Voyager 1, the farthest from home, and Parker Solar Probe, which flies closer to the Sun than any spacecraft before it. Their positions come from NASA/JPL's Horizons system, the same data scientists use.

Tap a spacecraft to see what it is, when it was launched and how far it is from you.

Asteroids passing Earth

Every week, small asteroids pass Earth. Wayaroo shows the ones passing soon, with how close they come and how big they are, using NASA/JPL's close-approach data. Most pass much farther away than the Moon, and the app tells you how far in plain numbers.

Meteor showers and tonight's sky where you are

Before you go outside, check tonight's sky for where you are:

  • When it gets dark, and how bright the Moon will be.
  • Which planets are up tonight.
  • The next meteor shower, such as the Geminids in December or the Perseids in August.
  • The chance of an aurora, and the nearest dark place to watch from.

Earth now, from space

See the newest pictures of the whole sunlit Earth from NASA's EPIC camera, on the DSCOVR spacecraft about 1.5 million km away, and from the NOAA GOES weather satellites, which watch the Americas and the Pacific from about 36,000 km up. Look for clouds, storms, smoke and snow.

Robots on Mars

NASA's rovers drive across Mars and send pictures home. Wayaroo shows where the rovers are on a map of Mars, such as Curiosity and Perseverance, with their newest pictures. A message from Mars takes between about 3 and 22 minutes to reach Earth, depending on where the two planets are.

Rocket launches, live on the map

When a rocket launches anywhere in the world, it shows on the map. Watch an animated flight rise from the launch pad, and open the next launches list: the rocket, the mission, the launch site and the countdown.

The flight on the map is an animation to show the launch. It is not the rocket's real path.

And down: famous dives under the sea

Space is up, the deep sea is down. Over deep water, dive from the surface to the real depth of the sea floor, past the ocean's zones and the animals that live in each. Follow famous dives into the deep, such as the first trip to the deepest point of the ocean, in 1960.

Games for a quick break

Waiting for the bus, or a launch? Wayaroo has chess, falling blocks, five in a row and four in a row, against the phone or with a friend on one phone. They are free, like the rest of Wayaroo, and they switch off while you drive.

More big numbers

  • 1.5million km

    How far away NASA's EPIC camera is when it takes its pictures of the whole Earth.

  • 3–22min

    The time a radio message takes between Mars and Earth. It depends on where the two planets are.

  • 11km

    About how deep the ocean is at its deepest point, in the Pacific. That is deeper than Mount Everest is tall.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. Wayaroo is free, needs no account and works in your browser at app.wayaroo.com, on iPhone and iPad, and on Android.

Are the planets really where the app shows them?

Yes, for the date shown. Wayaroo works out where the Sun, the Moon and the planets are from NASA/JPL's orbit numbers. Spacecraft positions come from NASA/JPL Horizons. Sizes and some distances are drawn bigger so you can see them.

Is the rocket on the map the real flight?

No. The flight on the map is an animation to show that a launch is happening. The launch times and details come from Launch Library 2 by The Space Devs, and launch times often change.
