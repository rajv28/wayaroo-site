Directions that look after you.

Wayaroo gives turn-by-turn directions by car, bike or on foot, with lane arrows before each turn and the arrival time that updates as you go. What makes it different is what it warns you about, out loud, before you get there.

Speed limit on your road

The limit for the road you're on where it's mapped, including school zones at the right times, with a quiet tone when you're over.

Level crossings

Whether the boom gates are likely to be down when you arrive, and roughly how long you'll wait, from the live trains.

Speed humps and crash hotspots

Spoken before a run of humps, and before intersections with a history of crashes.

Long traffic lights

Wayaroo learns how long each light usually takes and tells you when it's a long one, so you can relax instead of guessing.

Truck and heavy vehicle mode

Type your height and weight once; low bridges and load limits on the route are called out 2 km and 500 m ahead.

Share my trip

One tap sends a private link that shows where you are and when you'll arrive. It stops when you do.

Fuel, parking and rain, worked out for you

Before a longer drive, Wayaroo tells you if rain is likely at your destination when you arrive. In Melbourne's CBD, tap a parking bay to see the rule in force right now ("2P until 6:30 pm", "loading zone until 4 pm") from the council's sign data; the sign at the kerb always wins, so check it. And the fuel card says whether to fill up now or wait, from your area's price cycle.

Nothing distracting while you drive

No ads, no pop-ups, no moving weather pictures while navigation is on. The map tidies itself to just the road, the turns and the warnings. Voice prompts come in your language, and you can keep only the warnings or switch the voice off.

Drive recorder

Wayaroo notices when you're driving and keeps a record of each drive's distance and fuel cost on your phone, so you know what your car really costs. Nothing leaves the phone.

Tell me when it's out

Speed limits come from Victoria's Department of Transport and Planning and OpenStreetMap; level crossings and crashes from Victorian open data; parking rules from City of Melbourne sign data; limits for trucks from OpenStreetMap. Any of it can be out of date, missing or wrong: always follow the road signs and boom gates, and don't rely on Wayaroo at a level crossing. See the data credits.
