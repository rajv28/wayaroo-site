A car logbook for the ATO's logbook method: what to record

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 20 November 2026.

If you use your car for work, the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) lets you claim the work part of your car expenses with the logbook method. The method needs a logbook that obeys the ATO's rules. This page explains what the ATO asks for, what Wayaroo's logbook file gives you, and what you must still check and add yourself.

What the logbook method is

With the logbook method you claim the work-related part of your real car expenses, for example fuel or electricity, servicing, registration, insurance and the decline in value of the car. Your logbook gives your work-related use percentage: the work kilometres divided by all the kilometres of the logbook period, multiplied by 100. You then claim that percentage of your car expenses. You must also keep receipts or other records of the expenses.

What the ATO asks a logbook to show

  • At least 12 continuous weeks that are broadly representative of your travel.
  • For every work journey: the reason, the destination, the odometer reading at the start and at the end, and the kilometres travelled.
  • For the whole period: the odometer readings at the start and at the end, and the total kilometres.
  • When to write it: at the end of each journey, or as soon as possible after it.

A logbook stays valid for 5 years, unless your use of the car changes, for example when you change jobs or move house. In each of the next 4 years, you keep the odometer readings for the start and the end of the period you owned the car in the income year, and your work kilometres and work use percentage from the logbook. Keep the logbook and the odometer records for 5 years after the last income year in which you use them. For two or more cars, keep a logbook for each car, for the same period. Without a valid logbook you cannot use the logbook method; you may be able to use the cents per kilometre method instead.

What Wayaroo's logbook file gives you

Wayaroo records your drives on your phone. In Spend, open "📒 Car logbook (ATO)".

  • Work or personal. Mark each drive as work 💼 or personal 🏠 with one tap. Do this when the car is parked, not while you drive.
  • One odometer reading. Type your car's odometer reading at your first drive of the last 12 weeks.
  • The file. Tap "Export the logbook". Wayaroo saves a CSV file that a spreadsheet or a tax agent can open. It has the period, the odometer readings at the start and the end, the total and work kilometres, the work use percentage, and one line for each drive: the date, the odometer readings, the kilometres, work or personal, and columns for the reason and the destination.
  • A warning. When the drives cover less than 12 weeks, Wayaroo says so.
  • Private. Nothing leaves your phone. Wayaroo does not send the logbook anywhere.

What you must still do

  • Check the odometer readings. Wayaroo calculates each reading from the one reading that you typed and the kilometres that its GPS recorded. These are not your car's own readings, and GPS kilometres can be different from the odometer. The ATO asks for odometer readings: compare the file with your car's odometer and correct it.
  • Add the reason and the destination of each work drive, soon after the drive. For a drive that you did not plan in Wayaroo, the file leaves the destination empty: Wayaroo never makes one up.
  • Add the drives that Wayaroo did not record, for example when your phone was not in the car or the app was closed. Your total kilometres must include them.
  • Keep your receipts and the other records that the ATO asks for.

The logbook is yours, and you are responsible for what you claim. Wayaroo is not connected with the ATO. You can also keep an electronic logbook with the myDeductions tool in the ATO app. If you are not sure, ask a registered tax agent.
