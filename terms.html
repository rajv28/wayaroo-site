1. Wayaroo is a guide only

Wayaroo shows information to help you plan: live vehicle positions, timetables, routes, travel times, level-crossing and crash-hotspot warnings, pedestrian and school crossings, school zones, wildlife areas, speed humps, sharp bends, unsealed roads, water crossings, cattle grids, traffic-light waiting times, speed limits, cameras, road closures, fuel and trip costs, area figures, weather and more. Most of it comes from third parties and can be wrong, incomplete, out of date or missing. Estimates (for example how long boom gates will be down, how long you will wait at a traffic light, how late a bus is, or what a trip costs) are guesses.

Always follow road signs, signals, boom gates, traffic police and the road rules – they override anything Wayaroo shows. A missing warning does not mean there is no hazard. Traffic-light waiting times are worked out from how long other drivers and buses waited there before; they do not come from the traffic-signal system and are not a signal to go. Move only when the light is green and the way is clear. Check important information with the official source before relying on it.

2. Driving safely

This applies in the iPhone and Android apps, on the website and on car screens (Apple CarPlay). Set up your trip before you start driving. Don't hold or touch your phone while driving unless the road rules in your state allow it (for example a phone fixed in a commercially designed mount). Wayaroo speaks warnings so you can keep your eyes on the road. Never use Wayaroo in a way that distracts you or puts anyone at risk. Learner and P-plate drivers may not be allowed to use a phone at all while driving.

4. Area information

Don't use them as the only basis for decisions about where to live, buy or rent; get independent advice.

7. Parking, trucks, planes and the rest

Truck and heavy-vehicle warnings use height and weight limits that volunteers have mapped and can be missing or wrong: you must check the road signs and your own vehicle's limits, and Wayaroo is not an approved heavy-vehicle navigation system. Aircraft positions and landing times come from hobbyist receivers and are estimates for curiosity only, not for aviation or anything safety-related. If you share your trip, anyone with the link can see where you are until you stop. AI answers (off until you turn them on) can be wrong, also when they are about your trip; the road signs and the road rules apply, not the answer.
