Melbourne's Free Tram Zone

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 22 November 2026.

In central Melbourne you can ride any tram for free. You do not need a ticket, a myki card or an app, as long as your whole trip starts and ends inside the Free Tram Zone. Visitors and locals use it every day to cross the city. Here is how it works, and how to avoid paying a fare by mistake.

Where the zone is

The Free Tram Zone covers the central city and Docklands. In the city it is roughly the grid between Flinders Street, Spring Street and La Trobe Street, and it also takes in Queen Victoria Market and the Docklands waterfront. Tram stops inside the zone have Free Tram Zone signs, and on board the trams announce the last stop in the zone. Transport Victoria publishes the exact map.

When it is free, and when you need a myki

  • Both ends inside the zone: free. Do not touch on.
  • One end outside the zone: you need a myki and must touch on as normal, for the whole trip.
  • Touched on by mistake inside the zone? Touch off before you leave the zone. Transport Victoria says you must touch off inside the zone to be eligible for a reimbursement.
  • Trams only. The free zone does not include trains in the City Loop or buses. Those need a myki.

The City Circle tram

Route 35, the City Circle, is a free tourist tram that runs a loop around the city centre and Docklands with old W-class trams and a recorded commentary about the places it passes. It is part of the Free Tram Zone. The W-class trams are not wheelchair accessible, and the route does not run all day, so check the timetable. For a quick trip across the city, the regular trams on Swanston, Elizabeth, Collins and Bourke Streets are usually faster and more frequent.

Tips

  • Peak hours are busy. Trams along Swanston Street and Bourke Street can be full between about 8 and 9 am and 5 and 6 pm. Walk two blocks or wait for the next one.
  • Stand clear of the doors and move inside. Trams stop often in the city.
  • Hold on. Trams brake hard for cars and people.
  • Driving? In the city centre, some right turns are hook turns, made from the left lane to keep the trams moving. See how to do a hook turn.
