Thunderstorm asthma in Victoria

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 20 November 2026.

On some spring days in Victoria, a thunderstorm can cause sudden breathing problems for thousands of people at the same time. This is called epidemic thunderstorm asthma. It can affect people who have never had asthma. Victoria has a forecast for it every spring, and knowing how to read it can keep you and your family safe.

What happens

In late spring, the air in Victoria can carry a lot of grass pollen, mostly from ryegrass. A certain uncommon type of thunderstorm draws pollen up into the cloud, where it breaks into tiny pieces. The gusty winds in front of the storm then blow these small pieces down to ground level. They are small enough to go deep into the lungs, and people can have an asthma attack within minutes.

The forecast

  • When: every year from 1 October to 31 December, the grass pollen season.
  • How far ahead: three days: today, tomorrow and the day after. Today's forecast is updated once, at about 12:30 pm.
  • Who issues it: the Victorian Department of Health, published on VicEmergency.
  • Where: nine districts: Central (which includes Melbourne), East Gippsland, Mallee, North Central, North East, Northern Country, South West, West and South Gippsland, and Wimmera.
LevelWhat it means
Low (green)An event is unlikely.
Moderate (orange)One of the needed conditions may be present: a moderate chance.
High (red)High grass pollen and severe thunderstorms with strong winds are likely together.

The forecast is not a formal warning. A low forecast does not promise that nothing will happen.

Who is at risk

  • People with asthma now, or who had asthma in the past.
  • People with asthma that has never been diagnosed.
  • People with springtime hay fever, even without asthma.

The risk is highest for people who have both asthma and hay fever, and for people whose asthma is not well controlled.

What to do on a high-risk day

  1. Stay inside before and during the storm, especially when the wind gusts arrive in front of it. Close windows and doors. Turn off air conditioning that brings in air from outside.
  2. Keep your reliever puffer with you, as your doctor or pharmacist advised.
  3. Follow your asthma action plan if you have one, and know the four steps of asthma first aid.
  4. Call Triple Zero (000) if someone cannot breathe well, cannot speak in sentences, or their reliever does not help.

The best protection is good everyday asthma and hay fever care. Talk to your doctor before spring.

How Wayaroo helps

From 1 October to 31 December, Wayaroo reads the Department of Health's forecast for the district you are in, and the pollen card on the For you tab shows it. The forecast comes from VicEmergency's public data (CC BY 3.0 AU).
