Roundabout rules in Victoria

Written by the Wayaroo team, Melbourne. Updated 22 November 2026.

Roundabouts keep traffic moving and cut serious crashes, because cars meet at low speed and at a narrow angle. But many drivers are unsure about the rules, especially about indicating. Here are Victoria's roundabout rules in plain words.

The basics

  • Keep to the left of the central island. In Australia, traffic goes around a roundabout clockwise.
  • Follow any signs and arrows on the road before and inside the roundabout.
  • A roundabout is an intersection, even when there is no roundabout sign at the entrance.

Who gives way

Before you enter a roundabout, you must give way to:

  • any vehicle already in the roundabout, which in practice means vehicles coming from your right; and
  • any tram that is entering or approaching the roundabout.

Giving way means slowing down or stopping to avoid a crash. You do not have to give way to pedestrians at a roundabout unless there is a pedestrian crossing, but you should give people the time and space they need to cross.

Indicating and lanes

GoingIndicateLane (multi-lane roundabout)
LeftLeft as you approach, until you have left the roundaboutApproach in the left lane and stay in it
Straight aheadNo signal on the way in; signal left as you exit, if you canAny lane, unless it is marked for turns only; stay in your lane
Right (or a U-turn)Right as you approach; if you can, change to left just before your exitApproach in the right lane and stay in it

The left signal before your exit matters: it tells drivers waiting to enter that you are leaving, so they can go sooner.

Bikes, e-scooters and horses

People riding a bicycle, an e-scooter or a horse may stay in the left lane of a multi-lane roundabout even when they go straight ahead or turn right. They must give way to any vehicle leaving the roundabout. If the roundabout has a bike lane, riders must use it unless that is not practical. Drivers should expect a bike in the left lane to keep going around.

Common mistakes

  • Entering when a car is already coming around from the right. Wait for a clear gap.
  • Changing lanes inside the roundabout. Choose your lane before you enter.
  • Indicating right to go straight ahead. It tells other drivers the wrong thing.
  • Forgetting the exit signal. Signal left just before you leave, when you can.
